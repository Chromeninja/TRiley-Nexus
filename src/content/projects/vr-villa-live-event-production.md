VR Villa is not just a booth presence. It is a traveling, community-first VR program designed to make immersive gaming approachable for players at conventions and pop-culture events across the country.

My work focuses on the live production layer that makes that experience feel reliable in public. That means turning a rotating mix of venues, staff, hardware, games, and partner expectations into an event system that stays consistent from show to show.

At the operational level, the work includes staffing coverage, role assignments, pre-show readiness, floor coordination, staff coordination, payroll workflows, and attendee flow. On site, the experience is intentionally hands on: players check in, complete a waiver, choose from a curated game lineup, get coached through VR onboarding, and cycle through play sessions with clear turnover and sanitation practices between uses.

The role also sits at the intersection of multiple partner groups. VR Villa depends on alignment between event organizers, developers, hardware partners, sponsors, and on-site staff, so production has to support both audience experience and partner value at the same time.

What makes the model work is that it protects the mission while staying operationally practical. The activations remain free to play, approachable for first-time users, and useful to developers who want genuine player feedback and live visibility. My contribution is helping that mission survive the real constraints of touring production, including events that can scale up to 30 staff members and have reached 54K+ players across a 100+ activation history, so each event feels organized, welcoming, and worth returning to.
